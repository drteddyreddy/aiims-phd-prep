# 05 — Allen Carr component map (alcohol books)

Rewritten 5 Nov 2026 by the cloud session from the two paraphrased maps the local session produced (`carr-maps/quit-drinking-without-willpower.md`, `carr-maps/easy-way-to-control-alcohol.md`). Main source: *Quit Drinking Without Willpower* (QDWW, 20 chapters, current edition). *The Easy Way to Control Alcohol* (EWCA, 27 chapters) is used where it adds wording, especially on detox, withdrawal and medication. Paraphrase only; no new quotations. Mapping-row numbers refer to `thesis/03-lane2-belief-reversal-intervention.md` section 2 (rows 1–16 after this revision). **No human has checked the maps chapter by chapter**; verify any chapter citation that a protocol decision rests on.

The cannabis book (*Easy Way to Quit Cannabis*, Carr & Dicey 2022) was mapped first as a proxy. It shares the architecture (little/big monster, illusions, numbered instructions, final ritual, vow) and adds two concrete components the alcohol books state less explicitly: a written record of harms carried on the person (its instruction 6) and a pre-planned response to future pangs. Its hostility to anti-anxiety medication (Ch 9) is stronger than anything in the alcohol books. The proxy table has been dropped.

## 1. Architecture of the alcohol method (QDWW)

| Phase | Chapters | What happens | Mapping rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Set-up | 1–2 | Seven reading instructions; the "combination lock"; scepticism and open mind; failed attempts reattributed to the method | 5, 8, 11 |
| Diagnosis | 3–5 | Addiction as a mental trap; the void; Little Monster (withdrawal) and Big Monster (belief); pitcher-plant analogy | 1, 2, 3 |
| Dismantling | 6–7, 11 | Taste as tolerance; social, relaxation, habit and personality excuses; normal drinkers are in the same trap; cutting down raises craving | 2, 3, 7 |
| Fear and willpower | 8–10, 13 | Fear of failure and of success; willpower as deprivation; addictive personality as effect not cause; list of genuine pleasures; do not suppress thoughts | 4, 5, 9, 11, 12 |
| Decision | 12, 14–17 | You are a non-drinker at the final drink; confess to loved ones; withdrawal reframed; checklist; quit now; final drink and vow; anticipate high-risk situations | 6, 7, 13, 15, 16 |
| Maintenance | 18–20 | Welcome the pangs; bad days are normal; seven instructions restated; hypnotherapy audio as an aid | 9, 13 |

EWCA follows the same arc over 27 chapters, with seven reading instructions (Ch 1) and sixteen quitting instructions (Ch 26), and a final-drink ritual with the most unpleasant spirit (Ch 27).

## 2. The illusions, grouped (both books)

1. Alcohol gives pleasure, taste, or a reward; taste is acquired (QDWW Ch 4, 6; EWCA Ch 6, 12, 21).
2. Alcohol relieves stress, steadies nerves, gives courage or confidence, helps social life (QDWW Ch 3, 7; EWCA Ch 13–15, 20).
3. Drinking is a habit, a personality trait, a genetic fate, or a product of hardship (QDWW Ch 1, 7, 10; EWCA Ch 18).
4. Quitting is hard, needs willpower, means lifelong deprivation and grief for a lost friend (QDWW Ch 1, 8, 9, 13; EWCA Ch 5, 10, 19).
5. Controlled or moderate drinking is possible; cutting down is a sensible step; "just one" is safe (QDWW Ch 11, 12; EWCA Ch 22, 27).
6. Withdrawal is severe, dangerous, or like serious illness; DTs are caused by withdrawal (QDWW Ch 15; EWCA Ch 25). **Clinically false for dependent drinkers; see section 5.**
7. Freedom requires a waiting period or a special day (QDWW Ch 12, 16).
8. Other drinkers are happy and in control; normal drinkers are different in kind (QDWW Ch 11, 17; EWCA Ch 4, 23).

## 3. Procedural instructions (QDWW numbered 1–7; EWCA reading 1–7 and quitting 1–16)

- Follow all instructions in order; keep an open mind; start with elation (QDWW 1–3).
- Keep drinking while reading, read sober, do not cut down (EWCA reading 5–6; QDWW Ch 1 allows continued drinking with an exception for people already abstinent).
- Never doubt the decision; ignore conflicting advice; quit now; never drink again (QDWW 4–7).
- Reframe pangs as the monster dying; do nothing about them; do not suppress thoughts of drinking (QDWW Ch 13, 15, 18; EWCA quitting 3–5).
- Write a list of genuine pleasures (QDWW Ch 13); remind yourself regularly how miserable drinking was (EWCA Ch 27).
- Confess to loved ones when ready (QDWW Ch 14); do not try to convert friends; never envy drinkers (EWCA quitting 11, 15).
- Go to pubs and parties straight away; do not change your lifestyle to avoid alcohol (EWCA quitting 10; QDWW Ch 16, 19).
- No substitutes (EWCA quitting 13) versus soft drinks for thirst and genuine pleasures as replacements (QDWW Ch 6, 11, 13; EWCA Ch 27). The books disagree.
- Anticipate high-risk situations and plan responses; include them in the vow (QDWW Ch 17; EWCA Ch 26).
- Final drink and vow (QDWW Ch 17; EWCA Ch 27); hypnotherapy audio afterwards, with warnings about driving and epilepsy (QDWW Ch 20).

## 4. What the alcohol books say about medication, detox, withdrawal, medical supervision, AA, controlled drinking, substitutes

- **Pharmacotherapy for AUD**: QDWW never mentions naltrexone, acamprosate, disulfiram or any medication for drinking. EWCA Ch 15 likens alcohol to Valium and painkillers as things that numb a warning signal. Neither book recommends or forbids anti-craving medication because neither knows it exists.
- **Detox and withdrawal**: physical withdrawal takes a few days; the body clears alcohol in about a week to ten days (QDWW Ch 5, 15). DTs and feverish withdrawal are "a myth" for most people, and severe reactions are blamed on the willpower method's psychological strain (QDWW Ch 15). EWCA Ch 25 says DTs are caused by drinking, not withdrawal. No book mentions seizures, tapering, medical screening, or when to seek help. Unreferenced.
- **Medical supervision**: not discussed. Doctors appear as people who say quitting is hard (QDWW Ch 1), whose warnings make quitting harder (EWCA Ch 5), or as experts who can be wrong (EWCA Ch 2). "Ignore all advice that conflicts with Easyway" (QDWW instruction 5).
- **AA and twelve-step**: well-meaning, with some good ideas; right that one drink can restart the cycle; wrong to require lifelong vigilance and the identity of permanent vulnerability (QDWW Ch 1, 12; EWCA Ch 1, 3).
- **Controlled drinking and cutting down**: rejected; cutting down raises craving; normal drinking and problem drinking are stages of one disease (QDWW Ch 11; EWCA Ch 22).
- **Substitutes**: see section 3; the books conflict.
- **Hypnotherapy**: an aid to absorbing the book, not the cure; warnings about driving, machinery and epilepsy (QDWW Ch 20).
- **Unreferenced claims**: 90% of adults fall into the trap, 10% never do; lifetime spending figures; the denial of withdrawal danger (QDWW Ch 1, 15).

## 5. Carr vs the Lane 2 protocol

| Conflict with safe care in a dependent, post-detox population | Book and chapter | Protocol departure |
|---|---|---|
| Keep drinking until the end, then stop abruptly with a final drink; no taper, no transition, no screening | EWCA Ch 1 reading instruction 5, Ch 27; QDWW Ch 1, 13, 15, 17 | Belief work only after medically supervised detox (admission days 5–12, CIWA-Ar low); final drink replaced by a commitment ceremony |
| Withdrawal is mild; DTs are a myth or caused by drinking; seizures never mentioned | QDWW Ch 15; EWCA Ch 25 | Honest withdrawal education in session 1; the Little Monster metaphor reserved for the post-detox residual urge |
| Benzodiazepines and anti-anxiety drugs disparaged (strongest in the cannabis book) | Cannabis book Ch 9; EWCA Ch 15 by analogy | Detox regimen and taper are clinical decisions outside the module; the module never comments on them |
| Anti-craving medication absent from the method; "ignore conflicting advice"; doctors' warnings make quitting harder | QDWW instruction 5, Ch 1; EWCA Ch 2, 5 | Naltrexone, acamprosate, baclofen, disulfiram retained in treatment as usual and framed as "a seatbelt, not a crutch"; the ignore-advice instruction dropped and replaced with "test every belief against your experience and the evidence" |
| Controlled drinking rejected; AA's lifelong-recovery framing criticised | QDWW Ch 1, 11, 12; EWCA Ch 22 | Total abstinence kept as the goal (matches dependence); no disparagement of AA or mutual-help; harm-reduction patients are not eligible for v1 |
| No substitutes (EWCA) | EWCA Ch 26 instruction 13 | Follow QDWW: soft drinks and genuine pleasures allowed and used actively |
| Hypnotherapy audio | QDWW Ch 20; EWCA relaxation close | Replaced by a 10-minute breathing close |
| "Easy", "instant cure", unreferenced statistics | QDWW Ch 1; EWCA Ch 1 | Claim limited to "removes the sense of sacrifice"; statistics removed |
| No personality, genetic or adversity vulnerability | QDWW Ch 1, 10; EWCA Ch 18 | "Vulnerability is real but does not make the beliefs true" |
| Single 5–7 hour seminar; family absent; no comorbidity screen | Official format | Five ward sessions, groups of 3–6, family in sessions 2 and 4, MoCA and psychiatric screening |

## 6. Components the alcohol books add to the original ten-row map (now rows 11–16 in `03`)

11. Reattribute past failure to the method, not the person (QDWW Ch 2, 9; EWCA Ch 10).
12. Written list of genuine pleasures (QDWW Ch 13) and a written record of harms carried on the person (EWCA Ch 27; cannabis book instruction 6).
13. Pre-planned response to pangs and high-risk situations (QDWW Ch 17–18; EWCA Ch 26 instructions 4–5).
14. Go into social and stressful situations straight away; do not reorganise life around avoidance (EWCA Ch 26 instruction 10; QDWW Ch 16).
15. Quit now, not on a "meaningless day"; no transition period (QDWW Ch 16).
16. Disclose to loved ones when ready; do not convert others; do not envy drinkers (QDWW Ch 14; EWCA Ch 26 instructions 11, 15).

Candidate rows not yet adopted: the emotional "void" and developmental insecurity (QDWW Ch 4); social modelling by celebrities and peers (QDWW Ch 4); internal inconsistency on the disease framing (QDWW Ch 11 vs 15; cannabis book Ch 9 vs 15); the commitment vow as a device (QDWW Ch 17).
