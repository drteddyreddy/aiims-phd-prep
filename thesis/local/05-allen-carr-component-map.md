## Illusions dismantled, in book order (paraphrased, with chapter)

1. Cannabis gives genuine pleasure or a high (Ch 3, 4, 5). The "high" is relief from withdrawal the drug itself causes.
2. Cannabis is a support or crutch for stress, relaxation, and coping (Ch 3, 4, 9, 12). It worsens the stress it appears to relieve.
3. Cannabis boosts energy or acts as a stimulant (Ch 4). Tiredness is the body asking for rest.
4. Cannabis makes users more creative, interesting, or cool (Ch 4). The drug is said to be the star's cover, not their source of appeal.
5. Cannabis cures shyness or builds confidence (Ch 6, 8). It produces over-confidence and removes self-awareness.
6. Cannabis makes social life and parties more fun (Ch 6, 8). Enjoyment comes from company and setting.
7. Cannabis blocks out problems (Ch 5). Problems persist and usually grow.
8. Cannabis dampens fear and nerves in useful ways (Ch 8, 9). Fear and nerves are protective signals.
9. Users can take it or leave it, or cut down to controlled use (Ch 6, 8, 10, 12). Cutting down fails because addiction escalates.
10. Cannabis use is just a habit (Ch 6). The compulsion is addiction and withdrawal-driven, not habit.
11. An addictive personality or genes cause addiction (Ch 10). Use, not personality, initiates the cycle.
12. Difficult circumstances cause addiction and make it harder to quit (Ch 10).
13. Quitting requires willpower, a painful ordeal, or a long withdrawal (Ch 3, 5, 7, 10). Withdrawal is mild and short.
14. Quitting means sacrifice and grief for a lost friend (Ch 14).
15. Quitting requires waiting for a time milestone or a set period (Ch 12, 15).
16. One joint is harmless (Ch 14, 15). A single use re-triggers the cycle.
17. Addiction is permanent or incurable (Ch 9, 10, 12). The book presents it as easily ended by stopping.
18. Dope relieves fear and anxiety generally (Ch 13). Fear is caused by the drug.
19. Meaningless days such as New Year are good times to quit (Ch 15).
20. Other users are to be envied (Ch 6, 10, 12).

## Procedural instructions (numbered as in the book), each with the book's stated reason (paraphrased) and chapter

1. **Follow all of the instructions.** The method is a combination; missing one jeopardises freedom (Ch 1).
2. **Don't attempt to cut down.** Control is not possible before the instructed point, and it is best to avoid attempting it (Ch 1; reinforced Ch 2, 8, 10).
3. **Start off in a happy frame of mind.** A positive mood supports the change in perception needed (Ch 2).
4. **Think positively.** Negative anticipation is unhelpful, and quitting is an achievement (Ch 2).
5. **Keep an open mind.** Beliefs about cannabis must be questioned, as the method is a form of counter-brainwashing (Ch 2).
6. **Make a written record of how cannabis affected your life.** Memory of pain fades, so a past-tense record counters rosy recall (Ch 11).
7. **Make a solemn vow never to take cannabis, or anything similar, again.** The vow marks the breaking point of the addiction cycle (Ch 15; ritual Ch 16).
8. **Never doubt your decision to quit.** Doubt re-opens craving and leaves the user miserable either way (Ch 17).
9. **Ignore anything you read or hear about cannabis that conflicts with Easyway.** Scepticism protects against contrary information (Ch 17).
10. **Never take cannabis again.** This is the decision the reader has already reached (Ch 17).

Unnumbered procedural steps:

- Read only when not under the influence of any drug, including alcohol. The book gives no detailed reason beyond the need for a clear head (Ch 1).
- If you want a joint while reading, have it. Do not use willpower to resist, because that would interfere with the method (Ch 1).
- Carry the written record and refer to it when needed. It records what was escaped (Ch 11, 14).
- When the thought "I want a joint" arises, respond with "Great, I'm free" rather than resisting. Thoughts do not matter; what you do with them does (Ch 7, 11, 14, 15).
- Do not try to suppress thoughts of cannabis, because suppression increases them (Ch 7, 11).
- Visualise the Little Monster weakening and dying, and keep that image with you (Ch 15).
- Complete the ritual of the final joint, visualise the triumph, and seal the vow (Ch 16).
- Plan in advance for high-risk moments such as natural highs, trauma, and being around users, and include them in the vow (Ch 16).
- Quit now rather than on a meaningful or symbolic date, because such dates encourage deferral (Ch 15).
- Enter stressful and social situations soon after quitting to prove freedom (Ch 15).
- Write a list of genuine pleasures enjoyed without cannabis (Ch 13).
- Disclose the addiction to loved ones when the timing is right, and judge that timing yourself (Ch 14).
- Do not envy other users, who are described as trapped (Ch 6, 10, 12).
- Do not drink more alcohol to replace cannabis (Ch 8).
- Ignore experts who advise cutting down (Ch 2, 9).
- Prepare a response to withdrawal pangs in advance, framing them as the monster's death throes (Ch 17).
- Contact Easyway with doubts or questions (Ch 17).

## What the book says about medication, medical help, withdrawal, other drugs, substitutes (paraphrase; chapter)

- **Anti-anxiety medication (benzodiazepines).** The book says drugs such as Valium and Xanax cause more problems than they solve, that tolerance builds, and that doctors who defend them are treating symptoms rather than causes (Ch 9). Note that the text presents no cited evidence for these claims.
- **Medical and professional support generally.** The book says experts who prescribe cutting down should be ignored (Ch 2), and that "so-called experts" add confusion (Ch 9). It describes the author's own approach as superior to the willpower method, without citing outcome data beyond the centre's refund rate (Intro).
- **Twelve-step programmes (NA and AA).** The book criticises the framing of powerlessness and lifelong vulnerability (Ch 10). It also says those programmes do valuable work and that the willpower method, which they represent, leaves users vulnerable (Ch 12).
- **Withdrawal duration and severity.** Withdrawal is described as very mild and nearly imperceptible (Ch 3, 5). Total withdrawal is stated as two to five days (Ch 5), and three to five days elsewhere (Ch 10). The book says physical damage may take time to repair (Ch 14). Note that the book gives no source for these figures.
- **Mixing cannabis with tobacco.** The book says the withdrawal from tobacco and cannabis is identical, and that mixing them does not make quitting harder (Ch 5). Note that this is asserted, not referenced.
- **Caffeine.** Heavy coffee use alongside cannabis is described as compounding the problem (Ch 4). Caffeine is not given its own quitting instruction in this book.
- **Alcohol.** Quitting alcohol is said not to be necessary. The key rule is not to substitute alcohol for cannabis (Ch 8). A serious drinking problem is a separate matter the reader may choose to address (Ch 8).
- **Other drugs.** Easyway is said to have been applied to cocaine, opioids, and others (Intro, Ch 1). Heroin use is used as an analogy (Ch 14). No drug-specific guidance is given.
- **Substitutes and gimmicks.** The book says no substitutes, such as replacement therapies or gimmicks, are needed (Ch 1). The author reports trying substitutes when quitting smoking, without success (Ch 1).
- **Cutting down as a substitute strategy.** The book rejects controlled use, the "odd one", and rules for limiting intake (Ch 2, 8, 10, 12).

## Components with no mapping row (candidate new rows)

1. **Withdrawal severity and time course.** The book's claims that withdrawal is mild and lasts days could be coded as a separate row, since it is distinct from the "little monster" row.
2. **Controlled use or cutting-down rejected.** Row 5 covers willpower, but cutting down is a separate strategy the book rejects at length (Ch 2, 8, 10, 12).
3. **Single-use lapse as reactivation.** The claim that one joint reignites dependence (Ch 14, 15) is distinct from row 8.
4. **Cues, routines, and habit.** The habit-versus-addiction distinction and the parking-space analogy (Ch 6, 11) describe cue-driven re-engagement, which has no row.
5. **Reframing negative affect as a signal.** Tiredness, pain, nerves, and stress are described as warning systems (Ch 8, 9). This is an adaptive-appraisal component.
6. **Disclosure to family and friends.** The book's guidance on coming clean (Ch 14) has no row.
7. **Timing of quit attempts.** Meaningless days and trauma-triggered quitting (Ch 15) are a distinct component.
8. **Anticipatory relapse planning.** Preparing for natural highs, trauma, and users around you (Ch 16) extends row 9 but is a separate planning component.
9. **Reattribution of past failure.** Blaming the method rather than the person (Ch 7) is distinct from row 5.
10. **Social comparison and envy of other users.** Envy of dabblers and users is discouraged (Ch 6, 10, 12).
11. **Tolerance and dose escalation.** The book says tolerance drives escalation (Ch 8), which has no row.
12. **Shame, self-loathing, and guilt.** The book's emphasis on reducing self-blame (Ch 4, 15) is a distinct affective component.
13. **Fear of the unknown or of success.** Chapter 13 frames it as a separate fear domain from fear of withdrawal.
14. **Outcome guarantees and claims.** The money-back guarantee and refund-rate claims (Intro) and the Vietnam-veteran comparison (Ch 9) are persuasive or evidential claims a researcher will want to code separately.
15. **Internal inconsistency on the disease framing.** The book calls cannabis addiction a disease (Ch 15, 16) but also says addiction is not a permanent illness (Ch 9). This matters for any intervention claim about the construct.

## Points that matter for Lane 2 (my reading)

- **Benzodiazepines.** The cannabis book is hostile to anti-anxiety medication (Ch 9). In an alcohol-detox population that stance is unsafe. `03-lane2` §3 already departs from Carr here; the alcohol book's wording is still needed for the departure table.
- **Withdrawal framed as trivial** (2–5 days, "nearly imperceptible"). Fine for cannabis, wrong for alcohol (seizures, DTs). The intervention must begin after medical detox, which matches the pack's design.
- **Rejection of twelve-step framing and of cutting down.** This matches the pack's "no controlled drinking" choice. Note the tension with harm-reduction framing if the department uses it.
- **Instruction 6 (written record of harms, carried on the person)** and the **pre-planned response to future pangs** are concrete, manualisable components. Candidate session activities.
