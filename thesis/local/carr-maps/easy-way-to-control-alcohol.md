> **Read first (research-design safety flag):** This book claims alcohol withdrawal is nearly imperceptible, that delirium tremens is caused by drinking rather than withdrawal, and that drinkers should keep drinking until they finish reading and then stop abruptly with a "final drink." It does not mention seizures, medical detoxification, or screening for dependence. Clinically, alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous and sometimes fatal in dependent drinkers. Any intervention built from this method needs medical screening, a withdrawal-risk protocol, and an ethics review before recruitment. The analysis below reports what the book says, and the medication section flags where its claims conflict with established clinical knowledge.

## Procedural instructions (numbered as in the book if numbered), each with the book's stated reason (paraphrased) and chapter

**Reading instructions (Ch1; also Appendix A)**

1. Follow all the instructions. Reason: success depends on the full sequence, like a combination lock (Ch1).
2. Don't jump the gun. Reason: the book is built like a detective story, and later sections only make sense in order (Ch1).
3. Start in a happy frame of mind. Reason: a positive state makes the process easier, comparable to diving courage (Ch1).
4. Think positively. Reason: the author says positive thinking helps but is not enough alone (Ch1).
5. Do not quit or cut down until you have finished the book. Reason: continued drinking is said to allow the reader to test the illusions for themselves (Ch1; expanded Ch21, Ch16). Note: the book makes an exception for those already in recovery or abstinent for a day, who should stay abstinent if possible (Ch1).
6. Read only when sober. Reason: concentration and clarity are needed (Ch1).
7. Keep an open mind. Reason: the method depends on challenging lifelong beliefs (Ch1, Ch2).

**Quitting instructions (Ch26; abbreviated in Appendix B)**

1. Start from "Isn't it great? My life is no longer dominated by it," not from deprivation. Reason: thinking of deprivation creates misery and craving (Ch26).
2. Once you have made the correct decision, never question it. Reason: questioning revives craving and creates a losing position either way (Ch26).
3. Do not try to avoid thinking about not drinking. Reason: suppression rebounds; thinking about freedom is pleasant (Ch26).
4. Expect a small "little monster" for a few days and treat it as the goblin dying. Reason: the empty feeling is minor and can be reframed as a sign of freedom (Ch26).
5. Don't worry if you occasionally forget you have quit. Reverse any "I want a drink" feeling immediately. Reason: the pangs are adjustment to a change, not a sign of relapse (Ch26).
6. Don't wait to become a non-drinker. Reason: waiting to see if you'll drink again keeps you in doubt and treats freedom as a future event (Ch26).
7. Accept good days and bad days. Reason: bad days are blamed on quitting when they are ordinary, and the drink never made people happy (Ch26).
8. Recognise that you control the craving, not the other way round. Reason: the craving is in the brain, and you can choose how to interpret it (Ch26).
9. Do not mourn. Treat the drink as an enemy who has died. Reason: grief keeps the brainwashing and vulnerability alive (Ch26).
10. Don't change your lifestyle purely because you have quit. Go to pubs and parties straight away. Reason: avoidance implies the drink is needed (Ch26).
11. Don't try to convert friends unless they ask. Reason: arguments provoke resistance and make quitting harder for them (Ch26).
12. Change the parts of your lifestyle you don't like, for selfish reasons. Avoid drinking friends only where the shared activity was just drinking. Reason: time, energy, money and love are better spent on genuine pleasures (Ch26).
13. Do not use substitutes (activities, non-alcoholic drinks, food). Reason: substitutes signal a sacrifice and imply the drink filled a void (Ch26).
14. Break false associations (weddings, holidays, golf, meals without alcohol). Reason: these occasions were always enjoyable in their own right (Ch26).
15. Never envy drinkers. Reason: drinkers are deprived of health, money and freedom, and are addicted (Ch26).
16. Enjoy life. Reason: this is the purpose of the method (Ch26).

**Other instructions and checks**

- Pre-instruction checks (Ch25): confirm there are no benefits to drinking; confirm why cutting down is not an option (all or nothing); confirm there is no inherent physical or mental flaw. Reason: any "no" means re-reading before quitting (Ch25).
