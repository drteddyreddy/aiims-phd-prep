## Procedural instructions (numbered as in the book, then unnumbered steps)

**Numbered instructions in the book**

1. **Follow all the instructions in order.** Reason given: the method works like a combination lock, and skipping or reordering steps leaves the lock closed. (Ch 1)
2. **Keep an open mind.** Reason given: people accept things because others told them so, and the book's illusions only lose their hold once seen differently. (Ch 2)
3. **Start with a feeling of elation.** Reason given: the mind should expect freedom, not hardship, because expecting an ordeal makes it more likely. (Ch 3)
4. **Never doubt your decision to quit.** Reason given: doubt keeps the Big Monster alive and invites relapse. If you struggle with understanding, reread the relevant chapters. (Ch 8)
5. **Ignore all advice and influences that conflict with Easyway.** Reason given: well-meaning advice often assumes that drinking gives pleasure or that quitting is hard, which keeps the trap in place. (Ch 10)
6. **Don't wait to quit. Do it now.** Reason given: waiting for a special day or a better moment keeps the drinker in the trap and reinforces the myth that quitting is difficult. (Ch 16)
7. **Never drink alcohol again.** Reason given: a single drink restarts the Little Monster and the cycle. The book treats this as the logical conclusion of the earlier steps. (Ch 18, reinforced Ch 19)

**Unnumbered procedural steps**

- **Read the book while sober, or finish drinking before the end.** Reason: understanding is impaired by alcohol. Readers may keep drinking while reading, but the book says the final steps need to be taken without alcohol. (Ch 1)
- **Keep skeptical and question what you have been told.** Reason: the reader's beliefs about alcohol were formed by others and need testing. (Ch 2)
- **Reframe the withdrawal feeling as the Little Monster dying.** Reason: the feeling is caused by drinking and will pass; treating it as a reward removes the urge to drink. (Ch 3, 15, 18)
- **Make a written list of genuine pleasures in life without alcohol.** Reason: this replaces the imagined pleasure of drinking with real ones. (Ch 13)
- **Do not try to avoid thinking about drinking.** Reason: suppression makes the thought more persistent. Instead, reframe thoughts of drinking as signs of freedom. (Ch 13, 19)
- **Accept any "I want a drink" thoughts as signs of freedom, not as a need.** Reason: the Big Monster should be dead and the urge is only the last trace of the old belief. (Ch 13, 18)
- **Do nothing about withdrawal pangs and let them pass.** Reason: acting on them, including by drinking, keeps the Little Monster alive. (Ch 15, 18)
- **Visualise the Little Monster weakening and enjoy starving it.** Reason: an active mental image makes the process feel positive and not like deprivation. (Ch 15)
- **Confess to people close to you when the time is right.** Reason: secrecy adds stress, and honesty helps relationships recover. The reader decides the timing. (Ch 14)
- **Use the RATIONALIZED checklist and reread chapters where understanding is missing.** Reason: a gap in understanding is the usual cause of doubt before the final drink. (Ch 16)
- **Take the final drink, then make a vow never to drink again.** Reason: a clear, marked endpoint replaces uncertainty about when the reader is "cured". (Ch 17)
- **Anticipate high-risk situations and plan the response in advance.** Reason: prepared responses stop a lapse in mood from becoming a relapse. (Ch 17, 18)
- **Go out into social situations and handle stress without drinking from the start.** Reason: proving to oneself early removes the fear of success. (Ch 16, 19)
- **Listen to the hypnotherapy audio after reading the book and drinking the final drink, in a relaxed environment.** Reason: relaxation is said to help the content settle; the book says the audio is not what stops drinking. (Ch 20)

## What the book says about medication, detox, withdrawal, medical supervision, AA/12-step, controlled drinking, substitutes

- **Medication:** The book does not discuss pharmacotherapy (for example, naltrexone, acamprosate or disulfiram) at all, and it does not recommend or reject medication. (Whole book; no explicit chapter)
- **Detox and withdrawal:** The book says that physical withdrawal takes a few days, and that the body clears alcohol and its toxins within about a week to ten days. (Ch 5, 15)
- **Withdrawal danger:** The book describes delirium tremens and feverish withdrawal as a myth for most people who quit, and says most drinkers who quit have no abnormal physical symptoms. It attributes severe reactions to the willpower method's psychological strain. This claim is unreferenced and conflicts with clinical knowledge that severe withdrawal, including seizures and DTs, can be dangerous. (Ch 15)
- **No transition period:** The book says quitting needs no withdrawal period and that freedom begins at the final drink. It does not advise tapering, medical screening, or a plan for severe withdrawal. (Ch 13, 15)
- **Medical supervision:** The book does not discuss medical supervision, contraindications, or when to seek emergency help. The only medical reference is a passing mention that the medical profession says quitting is hard. In Ch 2 a doctor advises a drinker to stop without elaboration. (Ch 1, 2)
- **Hypnotherapy:** The book describes hypnotherapy as an aid for absorbing the content. It says the hypnotherapy is not the cure, and gives warnings about driving, operating machinery, and epilepsy, advising consultation with a doctor if in doubt. (Ch 20)
- **AA and 12-step:** The book describes AA as well-meaning with some good ideas. It states that AA's approach requires living with temptation indefinitely, and that AA's view that one drink can trigger relapse is correct. It rejects AA's framing of lifelong recovery. (Ch 1, 12)
- **Controlled drinking:** The book rejects controlled drinking and "cutting down" as options, arguing that they increase craving and keep the drinker addicted. It states that "normal" drinking and problem drinking are stages of one disease. (Ch 11)
- **Substitutes:** The book does not recommend non-alcoholic drinks as a strategy. It gives an anecdote in which the author drank fruit juice at a holiday ritual, and notes that a soft drink satisfies thirst better than beer. It suggests replacing drinking with genuine pleasures such as golf, walking and social contact. (Ch 6, 11, 13)
- **Other "help" and gimmicks:** The book lists hypnosis, acupuncture and laser therapy among gimmicks that drinkers have tried and failed with. (Ch 1)

**Research note:** Many of the book's central claims are unreferenced. Examples include the 90 percent of adults who fall into the trap, the claim that 10 percent never do, the figures for lifetime spending, and the denial of withdrawal danger. For a psychiatry study, these should be treated as claims to be tested, not as established facts.
