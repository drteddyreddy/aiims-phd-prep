# 02 — Lane 1: Neuromodulation and craving

## 1. Where the field stands (Nov 2026)

### 1.1 Conventional rTMS in alcohol use disorder

| Study | Design | Protocol | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mishra et al. 2010, CIP Ranchi | Sham-controlled, n=45, single-blind | 10 Hz right DLPFC, 10 sessions | Craving reduced vs sham |
| Mishra et al. 2015, CIP Ranchi | Right vs left DLPFC, n=22 | 10 Hz, 10 sessions | Both reduced craving, no difference between sides |
| Raikwar et al. (India) | Sham-controlled, 60 inpatients | 10 Hz left DLPFC, 10 sessions | **Null** for craving |
| Belgers et al. 2022 | RCT, sham, recently detoxified, 1-year follow-up | HF right DLPFC, 10 days add-on | Reduced craving and alcohol use in the first months; small sample (**verify** n and effect sizes) |
| Hoven et al. 2023, *Addiction* | RCT, sham, n=80 detoxified inpatients | 10 Hz right DLPFC, 110% RMT, 60 trains, 10 consecutive workdays | **Null**: abstinent days over 6 months 124 (sham) vs 115 (active); craving (AUQ/OCDS) unchanged |
| Perini et al. 2020 | RCT, sham, n=56 | Deep TMS to insula | **Null** |
| Harel et al. 2022, *Biol Psychiatry* | RCT, sham, n=51 recently abstinent | Deep TMS H7 coil, mPFC/ACC, 15 sessions over 3 weeks + 5 maintenance over 3 months | % heavy drinking days 2.9% vs 10.6% (p=.037); craving lower; reduced dACC–caudate connectivity |
| Meta-analysis 2025 (PubMed 39665462) | Multi-session rTMS vs sham in AUD | Pooled | Craving SMD −0.79 (95% CI −1.53 to −0.04) immediately post-treatment, I² 93%; smaller at 3 months |

Reading of this table: right-DLPFC high-frequency rTMS has a short-lived craving signal and an unresolved effect on drinking. The two best-conducted trials (Hoven, Harel) point in different directions and used different targets. The field has moved toward (a) medial prefrontal / ACC targets, (b) theta-burst, (c) accelerated dosing, and (d) stimulating during a defined brain state.

### 1.2 Theta-burst stimulation

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| iTBS meta-analysis 2026, *Frontiers in Neurology* (9 sham-controlled RCTs, n=556) | Craving SMD −0.65 (95% CI −1.08 to −0.21), I² 81%. Effect driven by methamphetamine trials; no benefit for nicotine craving; abstinence data insufficient. Authors: evidence is substance-specific. |
| TBS in methamphetamine use disorder meta-analysis 2025 (PubMed 40757793) | Cue-induced craving SMD 1.04 (95% CI 0.16 to 1.92), 4 RCTs. |
| Right DLPFC cTBS pilot, AUD, published Jan 2026 (PMC12791151) | n=28 (16 active, 12 sham), 10 sessions; relapse over 12 months HR 0.21 (95% CI 0.07 to 0.63); fMRI response predicted relapse. Small, hypothesis-generating. |
| Left DLPFC iTBS during withdrawal, Shanghai 2025 | n=41 inpatients; lower craving and better working memory vs sham. (**verify** parameters) |
| NCT03932149 (Shanghai, multicentre) | iTBS vs sham 2:1, craving primary; protocol published, results pending. |
| NCT06060496 (US) | "Theta Burst Stimulation for Alcohol Use Disorder", sham-controlled, ongoing. |
| CTRI/2023/12/060572 (India, institution not shown in snippet) | Accelerated TBS at medial PFC in AUD with comorbid depression, fMRI-based, sham-controlled. Registered Dec 2023. **Find the registrant**: this is your nearest Indian competitor or collaborator. |

Reading: TBS in alcohol is at pilot stage worldwide. There is no completed sham-controlled TBS RCT in AUD from India. That is a clean gap.

### 1.3 Accelerated protocols

- Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy (SNT) in depression: 10 iTBS sessions/day × 5 days, 1800 pulses/session, 50-min intervals, fMRI-guided. FDA-cleared for depression, not addiction.
- Accelerated iTBS for methamphetamine (NCT06320366, Colorado): 40 sessions over 5 days, left DLPFC, inpatient, n=20, sham-controlled, follow-up 12 weeks.
- Accelerated right-DLPFC HF-rTMS in AUD (Herremans et al. 2015): 15 sessions over 4 days, open-label; no change in the craving circuit on fMRI, possible attentional-network effect.
- Accelerated deep TMS to dACC in AUD, Stanford pilot 2024 (*Brain Stimulation*, via company release; **verify**): reports target engagement and abstinence.
- Open-label accelerated iTBS in cocaine: 3 sessions/day, 60-min gaps, 10 days.

Reading: acceleration solves the single biggest practical problem of rTMS in India, which is outpatient attendance over 2–4 weeks. Your patients are physically on the ward for 10–14 days. That is the design advantage no outpatient centre has.

### 1.4 Other modalities

| Modality | Evidence | Feasibility at AIIMS Guwahati |
|---|---|---|
| tDCS | Indian sham-controlled trial 2025 (*Indian J Psychiatry*, n=149 severe AUD): lower craving at 1 and 3 months, relapse prevention. Belgian trial (n=125): tDCS + alcohol-cue inhibitory-control training raised abstinence. Bollen 2022 review: mixed. | Cheap, portable, home-use possible. Good fallback if TMS access is limited. |
| Deep TMS (H7 coil) | Harel 2022 positive. | Needs a BrainsWay H-coil system. Unlikely available. |
| Low-intensity focused ultrasound to nucleus accumbens | Mahoney et al. 2023, n=4, safe, acute craving reduction. | Not feasible. Cite as horizon. |
| tACS / temporal interference | No addiction trials of note. | Not for a thesis. |
| Personalised fMRI targeting | BeamF3 vs MRI-guided DLPFC differ by median 0.65 cm; neuronavigated DLPFC rTMS in smokers reduced cigarettes/day; functionally neuronavigated TBS to right IFG studied in smokers. | Possible as an imaging sub-study if MRI research slots exist. Not needed for the main trial. |
| Stimulation paired with a state (cue exposure, regulation-of-craving training) | Yale pilot: rTMS + regulation-of-craving training vs passive viewing (results pending). Smoking iTBS trial with cue video vs neutral video. One methamphetamine study found cue + SFG rTMS *increased* craving. | Cheap to add as a standardised task during stimulation. Mechanistically interesting; keep it fixed across arms rather than making it a factor. |

### 1.5 Safety specifics for a detox ward

- Rossi et al. 2021 (IFCN guidelines): seizure risk low with standard parameters and focal coils, even on CNS-active drugs. Alcohol withdrawal and benzodiazepine withdrawal are listed as conditions that substantially raise seizure risk. Overall TMS seizure rate < 1% (Stultz 2020).
- Practical rule: do not stimulate in the first 72 hours of withdrawal, do not stimulate during active benzodiazepine taper if avoidable, and never within 48 hours of a withdrawal seizure or delirium. Benzodiazepines also raise motor threshold and may blunt plasticity, which argues for starting after the taper. On a 10–14 day admission, that typically means stimulation window = detox day 7–12. Confirm with your ward's taper schedule.
- Screening: TMS Adult Safety Screen (TASS), history of seizures, head injury, metallic implants, cochlear implants, pacemaker; measure RMT with EMG not visual twitch if possible.
- Thiamine status, electrolytes, and sleep deprivation (a seizure risk factor) should be documented before each day's sessions.

## 2. Gaps a single-centre Indian PhD can credibly fill

1. **No sham-controlled theta-burst RCT in AUD from India.** (Only the unidentified CTRI 2023 registration.)
2. **Nobody has exploited the inpatient detox window for accelerated TBS in alcohol.** Hoven's null trial used once-daily sessions post-detox; Herremans's accelerated study was open-label and measured only fMRI.
3. **Laterality is unresolved in Indian data** (Mishra right positive, Mishra right=left, Raikwar left null). A three-arm design (iTBS left vs cTBS right vs sham) would be the first direct Indian comparison with sham.
4. **Mechanism is rarely measured alongside outcome.** Craving under cue exposure, cognitive control (Stroop, Go/No-Go, n-back), delay discounting, and heart-rate variability are cheap and almost never all collected.
5. **Durability.** Most trials stop at 4–12 weeks. You have a follow-up infrastructure (the CBD trial's) that could carry 24-week outcomes.

## 3. Five candidate research questions, ranked

| Rank | Question | Why this rank |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does **accelerated iTBS over the left DLPFC delivered during the inpatient detox window** reduce craving at end of stimulation and relapse to heavy drinking at 12 weeks, versus sham? | Novel timing, feasible retention, matches the strongest TBS signal (iTBS, prefrontal), fits a 10–14 day stay. |
| 2 | **iTBS-left vs cTBS-right vs sham** during detox: which direction of prefrontal modulation reduces craving and relapse? | Answers the Indian laterality contradiction; three arms raise the sample size by 50%. |
| 3 | Does **cTBS over right DLPFC** reduce 6-month relapse (replicating the 2026 pilot HR 0.21) in an adequately powered Indian sample? | Replication is valuable but the pilot is tiny and the mechanism story weaker. |
| 4 | Does **tDCS + inhibitory-control training** during detox reduce relapse? | Strong fallback if the TMS machine is not practically available; cheaper; already an Indian positive trial so less novel. |
| 5 | Does **stimulating during a regulation-of-craving task** add to iTBS alone? | Mechanistically the most interesting, but a 2×2 needs ~4× the resources. Save for a post-doc. |

Recommendation: write the synopsis around Question 1, with Question 2 as the "if recruitment allows" extension and Question 4 as the fallback in the risk register.

## 4. Draft protocol for Question 1

### Title
Accelerated intermittent theta-burst stimulation of the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex during inpatient alcohol detoxification: a randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled trial of effects on craving, cognitive control, and relapse.

### PICO
- **P**: Adults 18–60 with alcohol dependence (ICD-11 / DSM-5-TR severe AUD), admitted for inpatient detoxification, CIWA-Ar < 8 and benzodiazepine-free (or on a stable minimal dose per local protocol) for ≥ 48 h at the time of first stimulation.
- **I**: Accelerated iTBS, left DLPFC, 3 sessions/day with ≥ 50-min intervals, 5 consecutive days (15 sessions, 9,000 pulses). Session: standard iTBS 600 pulses (bursts of 3 pulses at 50 Hz repeated at 5 Hz, 2 s on / 8 s off, 190 s), intensity 90–100% RMT measured by EMG on day 1 (state 80% if your machine's output and tolerability dictate; justify). Target by BeamF3 (or neuronavigation if available). Patients watch a standardised neutral video during stimulation (keeps state constant across arms).
- **C**: Sham iTBS, identical schedule, sham coil (preferred) or active coil tilted 90° with surface electrodes delivering scalp sensation (second-best; state blinding assessment).
- **O**: see outcomes below.
- Both arms receive treatment as usual: detox regimen, thiamine, brief psychosocial intervention, anti-craving medication at discharge per clinician.

### Design
Parallel-group, 1:1, stratified block randomisation (strata: SADQ ≥ 31 yes/no; CBD-trial participant yes/no if nested), allocation concealment by central web randomisation or sealed opaque envelopes held by a third person; participants, outcome assessors, and statistician blinded; operator unblinded. Blinding integrity checked after session 1 and session 15 (guess arm + confidence).

### Inclusion / exclusion
Include: as in P; able to consent; resident within follow-up reach or reachable by phone; family contact available for collateral.
Exclude: withdrawal seizure or delirium this admission; any seizure history or epilepsy; head injury with loss of consciousness > 30 min; intracranial metal, pacemaker, cochlear implant; psychosis, bipolar I, current suicidality; other SUD except nicotine (cannabis permitted if not dependence; decide); severe hepatic decompensation (MELD > 21), unstable medical illness; pregnancy; current lithium or bupropion or other seizure-threshold-lowering drugs that cannot be paused (document); prior TMS.

### Outcomes
| Level | Measure | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Penn Alcohol Craving Scale (PACS) change from baseline to day 5 (end of stimulation) | D0, D5 |
| Key secondary | Relapse to heavy drinking (≥ 5 drinks/day men, ≥ 4 women, or any 2 consecutive days of drinking; prespecify one) by Timeline Follow-Back + collateral + breath alcohol at visits | 4, 8, 12 weeks (24 weeks if infrastructure allows) |
| Secondary | Time to first drink; percent heavy drinking days; OCDS; cue-induced craving VAS before/after a standardised 3-min alcohol-cue video; Alcohol Urge Questionnaire | D0, D5, 4, 12 wk |
| Mechanism | Stroop interference, Go/No-Go commission errors, 2-back accuracy, delay discounting (k); heart-rate variability (5-min resting) | D0, D5, 4 wk |
| Mood / sleep | PHQ-9 or HAM-D, GAD-7, PSQI | D0, D5, 4, 12 wk |
| Safety | AE/SAE log per session, headache VAS, scalp pain, hearing, seizure | Every session, every visit |
| Biomarkers (optional) | GGT, CDT or PEth if available | D0, 12 wk |
| Blinding | Guess arm + confidence | After session 1, after session 15 |

### Sample size
Craving SMD from the 2026 iTBS meta-analysis is −0.65 with wide CI; alcohol rTMS meta-analysis −0.79 with I² 93%. Power conservatively on d = 0.6: 44 per arm, inflate 20% for attrition → **55 per arm, N = 110**. If relapse is made co-primary (expect 60% sham vs 35% active at 12 weeks, from Hoven's base rate and the cTBS pilot's hazard ratio), 62 per arm → 78 per arm with attrition, N = 156. For a three-arm design on d = 0.6 with Bonferroni-adjusted α = 0.025: ≈ 53 per arm → 66 with attrition, N ≈ 200. If recruitment is < 6/month, run N = 110 and treat relapse as secondary.

Recruitment arithmetic: at 20 alcohol-detox admissions/month, 50% eligible, 60% consent → 6/month → 110 in ~18 months. Verify against the ward numbers (question 3 in `01`).

### Analysis
Intention-to-treat; mixed-effects model for repeated measures for PACS (arm × time, baseline covariate, strata); Cox model for time to first heavy-drinking day; logistic regression for relapse at 12 weeks with multiple imputation under MAR and a tipping-point sensitivity analysis; exploratory mediation (cognitive control change → craving change → relapse). Prespecify in a SAP uploaded to CTRI/OSF before unblinding.

### Timeline (from protocol approval)
- Months 0–3: IEC, CTRI, sham-coil procurement, operator training (ideally a week at a centre running TBS; CIP Ranchi, NIMHANS, or AIIMS Delhi), RMT and sham pilot on 5 volunteers.
- Months 3–21: recruitment and stimulation.
- Months 21–24: last follow-ups.
- Months 24–30: analysis and writing.

### Budget heads
Sham coil (if absent), EMG electrodes/consumables, TASS and scale printing, breathalyser and mouthpieces, cue-video production, biomarker assays if added, participant travel reimbursement for follow-ups, data manager time, publication charges.

### Risk register
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| No sham coil | Budget for one; interim option tilted-coil sham with blinding assessment; or switch to Question 4 (tDCS) |
| Machine owned by Neurology with limited slots | Written MoU; 3 sessions/day for 5 days needs ~2 hours/day of machine time for one patient; stagger 2 patients/week |
| Co-enrolment conflict with CBD trial | Decide relationship type (nested post-window vs parallel cohort) before synopsis |
| High treatment response in both arms (Hoven's problem) | Primary at D5 is proximal; relapse secondary; enrich sample with SADQ ≥ 31 |
| Benzodiazepine still on board at stimulation | Hard rule ≥ 48 h off, document dose; sensitivity analysis excluding any on benzodiazepines |
| Unblinding by scalp sensation | Sham coil; standard script; blinding index reported |
| Dropout after discharge | Phone TLFB, collateral informant, travel reimbursement, align visits with CBD-trial follow-ups |

## 6. Equipment and training checklist

- [ ] Confirm machine can deliver theta-burst (not all older units can run 50 Hz triplets at the required intensity; check duty-cycle limits and coil cooling for 3 sessions/day).
- [ ] Figure-of-eight coil, 70 mm.
- [ ] Sham coil matched to active coil, or plan the tilt + electrode method.
- [ ] EMG for RMT (APB or FDI), or document visual-twitch method as a limitation.
- [ ] Cap and BeamF3 measurement tape and calculator (nasion–inion, tragus–tragus, head circumference).
- [ ] Ear protection, emergency tray, seizure protocol posted in the room.
- [ ] Operator competence: hands-on week at an established centre; log 20 supervised sessions before first participant.
- [ ] Read now: Chen, Blumberger & Fitzgerald chapters on TBS parameters, RMT, and safety (already in your Drive); Rossi et al. 2021; Ekhtiari et al. 2019 consensus paper on NIBS in addiction.
