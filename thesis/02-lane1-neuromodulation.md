## 1. Where the field stands (Nov 2026)

### 1.1 Conventional rTMS in alcohol use disorder

| Study | Design | Protocol | Result | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mishra et al. 2010, *Addiction*, CIP Ranchi | Single-blind, sham-controlled, **2:1 allocation (30 active, 15 sham)**; entry CIWA-Ar ≤ 10 | 10 Hz right DLPFC, 20 trains of 4.9 s, 30 s ITI, 10 sessions; %RMT and sham method not in abstract | ACQ-NOW craving reduced vs sham; treatment × time η² = 0.401; relapse not measured | AB |
| Mishra et al. 2015, *J Neuropsychiatry Clin Neurosci*, CIP Ranchi | Right vs left DLPFC, double-blind, **n = 20** | 10 Hz, 20 trains, 10 sessions | Both sides reduced ACQ-NOW craving (time F = 185.9, η² = 0.91); no difference between sides | SEC |
| Raikwar et al. Reading: right-DLPFC high-frequency rTMS has a short-lived craving signal in small single-blind trials and no demonstrated effect on drinking in the one adequately sized trial. The two best-conducted trials (Hoven, Harel) differ in target and conclusion. The field has moved toward medial prefrontal targets, theta-burst, intensive dosing, and stimulation during a defined state.

### 1.3 Accelerated protocols

- Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy in depression: 10 iTBS sessions/day × 5 days, 1,800 pulses/session, 50-min intervals, fMRI-guided. Cleared for depression, not addiction.
- Rossi 2021 notes accelerated iTBS "with five sessions per day and 1620 pulses per session" and 120% RMT variants, and states it cannot be concluded that higher intensity or pulse numbers beat standard iTBS at 80% RMT or AMT.
- Accelerated iTBS for methamphetamine (NCT06320366, Colorado): 40 sessions over 5 days, left DLPFC, inpatient, n = 20, recruiting to 2028.
- **Herremans et al. 2015 (Belgium), the only accelerated protocol in detoxified AUD**: 15 sessions over **3 days** (4/5/5, 15 min apart), 20 Hz, 110% RMT, 1,560 pulses/session, MRI-guided right DLPFC, 23,400 pulses total; **started a mean 17.8 days (SD 6.0) after the last drink, after diazepam detox**; open-label, no sham. General craving fell (OCDS, AUQ, p = 0.02 each); cue-induced craving did not change; one left inferior-parietal fMRI cluster changed; two headaches. (FT)
- Accelerated iTBS in smoking (Mikellides et al. 2022, NCT05271175, n = 104): 20 sessions over 5 days, 600 pulses, 100% RMT, BeamF3; included in the Jiang meta-analysis.
- Open-label accelerated iTBS in cocaine: 3 sessions/day, 60-min gaps, 10 days.

Reading: acceleration solves the single biggest practical problem of rTMS in India, which is outpatient attendance over 2–4 weeks. Your patients are physically on the ward for 10–14 days. Herremans shows a 3-day accelerated course is tolerable in detoxified inpatients, but nobody has run one sham-controlled with drinking outcomes.

### 1.5 Safety specifics for a detox ward (Rossi et al. 2021, full text; Ekhtiari et al. 2019 consensus, full text)

- Rossi 2021 gives **no quantified seizure risk for alcohol, alcohol withdrawal or benzodiazepines**. It lists alcohol withdrawal among medical provoking factors, lists alcohol consumption, sleep deprivation and stress among general provoking factors, notes six alcohol-associated seizures among H1-coil psychiatric cases, and recommends documenting alcohol intake and other threshold-lowering factors during TMS.
- For TBS, Rossi reports the usual range as **80–100% RMT** and judges TBS in that range safe; the first TBS seizure was cTBS to M1 at 100% RMT; other TBS seizures exceeded those parameters; "for iTBS, the risk to induce seizures is very low". Protocols beyond Supplementary Table S5 should follow its section 6.1 (dedicated safety monitoring).
- Practical rules for this protocol: no stimulation in the first 72 h of withdrawal; start only after CIWA-Ar < 8–10 and **after the benzodiazepine taper, with the dose on the day recorded at every session**; never within 48 h of a withdrawal seizure or delirium; **intensity ≤ 100% RMT**; document sleep, alcohol intake, thiamine and electrolytes daily; TASS screening; EMG-determined RMT; emergency tray and seizure protocol in the room. Joshi's ≥ 3 days off alcohol and benzodiazepines and Herremans' mean 17.8 days are the two published anchors for timing.
- Ekhtiari 2019 recommendations worth citing in the protocol: target both craving and withdrawal early in abstinence because the first days carry relapse risk; define and report the localisation method; report blinding success for participants and staff; TLFB as the self-report standard, paired with biomarkers repeated at each biomarker's dissipation rate; **at least 3 months of follow-up**; add cognitive markers (Go/No-Go, N-back, delay discounting) and a functional measure (WHOQOL-BREF); single-item VAS craving is contested, so pair it with a multi-item scale.

## 2. Gaps a single-centre Indian PhD can credibly fill

1. **No sham-controlled theta-burst RCT in AUD from India.** (Only the unidentified CTRI 2023 registration.)
2. **Timing relative to detox is almost never reported.** Hoven: "within 4 months", interval unreported, no benzodiazepine policy. Zhao: unreported. Herremans: 17.8 days. Joshi: ≥ 3 days. Nobody has exploited the inpatient window with a sham-controlled accelerated TBS course and drinking outcomes.
3. **Laterality is unresolved in Indian data** (Mishra right positive, Mishra right = left, Raikwar left null at 120% MT with crude localisation).
4. **Mechanism is rarely measured alongside outcome.** Cue-reactivity craving, cognitive control, delay discounting, heart-rate variability and EEG (your guide's interest) are cheap and almost never all collected.
5. **The incumbent Indian result is tDCS, not TMS.** Joshi 2025 is single-blind, male-only, 3-month follow-up, and small on craving (d = 0.45). A double-blind replication-extension with longer follow-up is itself a legitimate PhD if the TMS route closes.

## 3. Five candidate research questions, ranked

| Rank | Question | Why this rank |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does **accelerated iTBS over the left DLPFC delivered during the inpatient detox window** reduce craving at end of stimulation and relapse to heavy drinking at 12 weeks, versus sham? | Novel timing and schedule; feasible retention; matches the strongest TBS signal (prefrontal iTBS); fits a 10–14 day stay. Must be positioned against Joshi 2025 (focality, double-blind, mechanism) and checked against Kinney 2026. |
| 2 | **iTBS-left vs cTBS-right vs sham** during detox | Answers the Indian laterality contradiction and the Zhao cTBS signal; three arms raise N by 50%. |
| 3 | Does **cTBS over right DLPFC** reduce 6-month relapse, replicating Zhao 2026 with a hard relapse definition and an adequately powered Indian sample? | The pilot is tiny with a soft outcome and a baseline imbalance; replication is useful but less original. |
| 4 | **Double-blind replication-extension of Joshi 2025** (bilateral tDCS twice daily × 5 days, inpatients only, 6-month follow-up, mechanism battery, both sexes) | Highest feasibility; no sham coil needed; directly builds on an AIIMS result; fallback if the TMS machine is not practically available. |
| 5 | Does **stimulating during a regulation-of-craving task** add to iTBS alone? | Mechanistically the most interesting; a 2 × 2 needs ~4× the resources. Post-doc material. |

Recommendation: write the synopsis around Question 1, with Question 2 as the "if recruitment allows" extension and Question 4 as the named fallback in the risk register. Read Kinney 2026 first.

## 4. Draft protocol for Question 1

### Title
Accelerated intermittent theta-burst stimulation of the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex during inpatient alcohol detoxification: a randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled trial of effects on craving, cognitive control, and relapse.

### PICO
- **P**: Adults 18–60 with alcohol dependence (ICD-11 / DSM-5-TR severe AUD, SADQ ≥ 31 preferred), admitted for inpatient detoxification, CIWA-Ar < 8 and **benzodiazepine taper complete** (or ≥ 72 h at a stable minimal dose per local protocol, dose recorded) at the time of first stimulation.
- **I**: Accelerated iTBS, left DLPFC, 3 sessions/day with ≥ 50-min intervals, 5 consecutive days (15 sessions, 9,000 pulses). Session: standard iTBS 600 pulses (triplets at 50 Hz repeated at 5 Hz, 2 s on / 8 s off, 190 s), **intensity 80–100% RMT by EMG on day 1** (Rossi's judged-safe range; justify the chosen value by tolerability and device output). Target by BeamF3 (or neuronavigation if available). Patients watch a standardised neutral video during stimulation so the state is constant across arms.
- **C**: Sham iTBS, identical schedule, **sham coil preferred**. Hoven's tilted coil was guessed correctly by 63%, so budget for a proper sham coil; if unavailable, tilted coil plus surface electrodes, with blinding assessed.
- **O**: below.
- Both arms receive treatment as usual: detox regimen, thiamine, brief psychosocial intervention, anti-craving medication at discharge per clinician (recorded and stratified).

### Design
Parallel-group, 1:1, stratified block randomisation (strata: SADQ ≥ 31; CBD-trial participant yes/no if nested; anti-craving medication planned yes/no), allocation concealment by central web randomisation or sealed opaque envelopes held by a third person; participants, outcome assessors and statistician blinded; operator unblinded. Blinding integrity after session 1 and session 15 (guess + confidence) for participants and assessors.

### Inclusion / exclusion
Include: as in P; able to consent after withdrawal clears; reachable for follow-up; family contact available.
Exclude: withdrawal seizure or delirium this admission; any seizure history; head injury with LOC > 30 min; intracranial metal, pacemaker, cochlear implant; psychosis, bipolar I, current suicidality; other SUD except nicotine (decide on cannabis); MELD > 21; unstable medical illness; pregnancy; seizure-threshold-lowering drugs that cannot be paused; prior TMS.

### Outcomes
| Level | Measure | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Penn Alcohol Craving Scale (PACS) change, baseline to day 5 | D0, D5 |
| Key secondary | Relapse to heavy drinking (> 60 g/day men, > 40 g/day women, Hoven's definition) by TLFB + collateral + breath alcohol | 4, 8, 12 weeks; 24 weeks if infrastructure allows |
| Secondary | Time to first drink; % heavy drinking days; OCDS (English/Hindi version as used by Joshi 2025, plus Assamese translation); cue-induced craving VAS before/after a 3-min alcohol-cue video; AUQ | D0, D5, 4, 12 wk |
| Mechanism | Stroop, Go/No-Go, 2-back, delay discounting; 5-min resting HRV; resting EEG if the department's equipment allows (guide's interest) | D0, D5, 4 wk |
| Mood / sleep / function | PHQ-9, GAD-7, PSQI, WHOQOL-BREF | D0, D5, 4, 12 wk |
| Safety | AE/SAE per session; headache VAS; scalp pain; hearing; seizure; **benzodiazepine dose on the day**; sleep hours; alcohol intake check | Every session, every visit |
| Biomarkers (optional) | GGT, CDT or PEth | D0, 12 wk |
| Blinding | Guess + confidence, participant and assessor | After sessions 1 and 15 |

### Sample size
State the source of the effect size honestly: the iTBS craving SMD of −0.65 comes from methamphetamine-dominated pooling with no alcohol craving trial in it; the alcohol rTMS SMD of −0.79 has I² 93%. Power conservatively on **d = 0.6: 44 per arm → 55 per arm with 20% attrition, N = 110**. Alternative basis from Joshi 2025's relapse rates (63.5% vs 44%): ≈ 102 per arm → 128 per arm with attrition, N = 256, which is beyond a single-centre PhD; so relapse stays secondary. Three-arm design on d = 0.6 with α 0.025: ≈ 53 per arm → 66 with attrition, N ≈ 200. If recruitment is < 6/month, N = 110 with relapse secondary.

Recruitment arithmetic: at 20 alcohol-detox admissions/month, 50% eligible, 60% consent → 6/month → 110 in ~18 months. Verify against ward numbers.

### Analysis
ITT; MMRM for PACS (arm × time, baseline covariate, strata); Cox for time to first heavy-drinking day; logistic regression for relapse at 12 weeks with multiple imputation under MAR and a tipping-point sensitivity analysis (Hoven's "missing = relapse" as the conservative sensitivity); exploratory mediation (cognitive-control change → craving change → relapse). SAP uploaded to CTRI/OSF before unblinding.

### Timeline (from protocol approval)
Months 0–3 IEC, CTRI, sham-coil procurement, operator training (a week at AIIMS Bibinagar with Dr. Tikka's group, or CIP Ranchi, or NIMHANS), RMT and sham run-in on 5 volunteers. Months 3–21 recruitment and stimulation. Months 21–24 last follow-ups. Months 24–30 analysis and writing.

### Budget heads
Sham coil, EMG consumables, breathalyser and mouthpieces, cue video, scales translation and validation (trilingual, required by the Guwahati IEC), biomarker assays if added, participant travel for follow-ups, data manager time, publication charges.

### Risk register
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| No sham coil | Budget for one; interim tilted-coil sham with blinding assessment; or switch to Question 4 (tDCS) |
| Machine owned by Neurology with limited slots | Written MoU; 3 sessions/day × 5 days ≈ 2 h/day of machine time per patient; stagger 2 patients/week |
| Machine cannot deliver theta-burst at the duty cycle needed | Confirm with the manufacturer before the synopsis; Question 4 as fallback |
| Kinney 2026 shows a null or a large effect that changes the premise | Read it first; adjust the question (e.g., timing or acceleration as the new variable) |
| Co-enrolment conflict with CBD trial | Decide relationship type before the synopsis |
| High TAU response in both arms (Hoven) | Proximal primary at D5; enrich with SADQ ≥ 31; stratify by anti-craving medication |
| Benzodiazepine still on board at stimulation | Hard rule; per-session dose log; sensitivity analysis excluding anyone on benzodiazepines |
| Unblinding by scalp sensation | Sham coil; standard script; blinding index reported |
| Dropout after discharge | Phone TLFB, collateral informant, travel reimbursement, align visits with CBD-trial follow-ups |

## 5. Systematic review to run first (paper 1): scope narrowed

Broad reviews of NIBS in AUD are already done or registered: Treiber 2025 (*J Addict Med*), CRD42024567484 (*BMC Psychiatry* 2025, NIBS for craving in AUD), CRD42024504362 (network meta-analysis protocol, *PLoS One* 2025), Jiang 2026 (*Front Neurol*, iTBS in SUD), and the medRxiv living review. Do not propose another broad one.

Surviving angle: **"Timing of non-invasive brain stimulation relative to detoxification in alcohol use disorder: a systematic review of when trials stimulate, what they report about withdrawal and benzodiazepines, and whether timing moderates craving and relapse outcomes."** It fills a documented reporting gap, needs no new pooled effect to be interesting, and sets up your trial's design choice. Register on PROSPERO after a manual check (the PROSPERO and PubMed searches could not be completed from the laptop; the scripted search strings are in `thesis/local/scripts/`). Alternative: an LMIC-focused review of NIBS in SUD, which also looks unregistered.

## 7. How Lane 1 yields three to four papers (check each journal against the IF > 1.5 gate)

2. Protocol paper (*Trials*, *BMJ Open*, *Asian J Psychiatry*).
