## 4. Indian regulatory frame

- **ICMR National Ethical Guidelines 2017**: governs both lanes. Alcohol-dependent inpatients are a population with potentially compromised autonomy during early withdrawal; consent must be taken after CIWA-Ar is low and cognition is clear, and re-confirmed.
- **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019**: an "academic clinical trial" (approved product, new indication, investigator-initiated, non-commercial) needs IEC approval, not CDSCO permission, provided results are not used for promotion. rTMS is a marketed device used off-label for AUD, so Lane 1 is an academic trial of a medical device. Document this reasoning explicitly in the protocol; IECs ask.
- **CTRI registration**: mandatory for regulatory trials, strongly recommended for all others, and required by ICMJE journals before first enrolment. Register both lanes prospectively. Behavioural trials are accepted.
- **SAE reporting**: ICMR 2017 aligns academic trial SAE reporting with NDCT 2019 timelines. You already do this for the CBD trial; reuse the SOP.
- **DPDP Act 2023**: if Lane 2 collects data through an app or WhatsApp, data-fiduciary obligations apply. Keep clinical data on institute servers; use the app only for content delivery and minimal engagement logs in the first version.

## 5. Interaction with the CBD trial (the constraint that matters most)

Three possible relationships between your thesis and the trial:

1. **Nested aftercare study.** Participants complete the CBD trial's inpatient phase; after its primary endpoint window they are offered your intervention (TMS or belief-reversal) as a post-detox study with its own consent. Pros: cohort, follow-up infrastructure, biomarkers already collected. Cons: needs steering-committee approval; CBD arm becomes a stratification variable; trial follow-up visits may already be heavy.
2. **Parallel cohort.** You recruit detox inpatients who are screened out of or decline the CBD trial, or admissions after the trial closes recruitment. Pros: no contamination. Cons: smaller flow; selection differs from trial participants.
3. **Co-enrolment during the trial's active window.** Avoid unless the PI explicitly agrees in writing. Any add-on intervention during the inpatient phase confounds the trial's primary outcome.

Ask the PI which of 1 or 2 is acceptable. The answer sets your recruitment rate and therefore your sample size feasibility.
