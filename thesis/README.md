## The bottom line, in six lines

1. **Both lanes are viable at AIIMS Guwahati.** You have the three scarce things: a continuous inpatient alcohol-detox cohort, a TMS machine, and a department already running a technology-for-continuity-of-care project in substance use.
2. **Lane 2 is the stronger thesis** on novelty, publication yield, independence from equipment, and fit with you. Nobody has trialled the Allen Carr logic in alcohol dependence, in a clinical post-detox population, in India, or with mechanism measurement. That is four firsts in one project.
3. **Lane 1 is the safer thesis** on committee acceptability and clarity of design, but its best version (accelerated iTBS during the detox stay) depends on a sham coil, machine time, and a co-enrolment agreement with the CBD trial.
4. **Recommended structure:** Lane 2 as the thesis (development + proof-of-concept + pilot RCT, four papers). Lane 1 as a secondary, smaller sham-controlled pilot run on the same ward in years 2–3, which also sets up the department's TMS research line.
5. **The one question that decides everything:** does your guide expect the thesis to sit inside the ICMR cannabidiol trial? Ask this week. Both lanes can be nested as post-detox aftercare studies on the trial's cohort if the steering committee agrees; neither can co-enrol participants during the trial's primary-outcome window without the PI's consent.
6. **Nothing here needs new approval to start.** The systematic review, the qualitative expectancy study, and the manual drafting for Lane 2 can begin now, before the synopsis is approved.
