# Questionnaire translation and validation plan (English, Hindi, Assamese)

Draft v0.1, 5 Nov 2026. Not yet reviewed by the guide.

**Why this is on the critical path.** The AIIMS Guwahati IEC checklist asks for **validated questionnaires in English, Hindi and Assamese** with the submission, not as an annexure to be done later (`05` §B; `local/04-aiims-rules.md`). So every scale in the main protocol must be translated, checked and piloted **before** the main IEC submission (month 9 from registration at the latest). The qualitative study (`qual-expectancy-protocol.md`) does not need these scales, so it can go to the IEC first while this work runs.

**Provenance warning.** The permission and existing-version columns below are my working knowledge, **not verified**. Each row must be checked against the developer's site or paper before relying on it, and the result recorded in the tracking sheet (§6). Only the Hindi OCDS row is verified (Joshi 2025 full text, `02-lane1` §1.4).

- **Hindi.**

- **Bengali:** not required by the IEC, but Barak Valley and Bengali-speaking patients are common in Guwahati. Decide after open question 7 (languages on the ward). If needed, add it to Tier 1 scales only.
- Script: Assamese in Assamese (Bengali–Assamese) script; Hindi in Devanagari. Check fonts render in REDCap and on printouts.
- **Literacy:** many patients may not read comfortably. All scales are **interviewer-administered** in the main study, so cognitive interviewing tests oral administration as well as reading.

## 3. Method (per scale, per language)

Based on Beaton et al. 2000 (cross-cultural adaptation of self-report measures) and the ISPOR principles for translation and cultural adaptation (Wild et al. 2005); reported with COSMIN guidance.

1. **Permission** from the developer for translation and use; ask whether an official translation exists or is under way, and whether the developer requires their own process.
2. **Forward translation** by two independent bilingual translators whose mother tongue is the target language: one with clinical knowledge, one lay. Written notes on difficult items.
3. **Reconciliation** of the two into one version by the translators plus the PI.
4. **Back-translation** into English by two translators blind to the original, with no clinical background.
5. **Expert committee**: PI, guide or co-guide, one psychologist, the translators, and one person in recovery (paid). Checks semantic, idiomatic, experiential and conceptual equivalence item by item; records every change in a decision log. Sends a summary to the developer if they ask for it.
6. **Cognitive interviewing**: 8–10 inpatients or recent outpatients per language, a mix of education levels. Think-aloud plus verbal probes (what does this question mean to you? how did you choose your answer?). Revise and retest problem items with 3–5 more people.
7. **Pre-final version** sign-off by the expert committee.
8. **Field test and psychometrics** (§5).

Time per scale per language: about 6–8 weeks for steps 1–7 if translators are lined up; several scales run in parallel.

## 4. Local adaptations that are not translation

- **Standard drink and beverage card for TLFB.** India has no single official standard drink; the protocol must define one (e.g., grams of ethanol per unit) and build a picture card of common local drinks: country liquor, IMFL spirits, beer (regular and strong), and **locally brewed rice beer**, whose strength varies by community and batch. Mark rice-beer conversions as approximate and analyse sensitivity to the assumption.
- **Expectancy content.** Beliefs found in the qualitative study but missing from CEOA/AEQ (e.g., alcohol for physical labour, cold, pain or ritual) go into a separate **supplementary local-expectancy module**, developed and tested alongside but scored separately. Never edit validated items to add them.
- **Relapse definition** in grams per day (`02-lane1` §4; `03` §8) must be convertible from the beverage card.

## 5. Psychometric testing

Field-test sample per language: **at least 100 respondents** for internal consistency and basic structure (more for confirmatory factor analysis of longer scales; COSMIN rates ≥ 100 as adequate for most properties). This is the main bottleneck: about 100 Assamese-speaking and 100 Hindi-speaking people with AUD. Recruitment sources: inpatients, de-addiction outpatients, and (with permission) other centres in Guwahati.

| Property | Method | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Internal consistency | Cronbach's α and McDonald's ω | Report per subscale |
| Structure | CFA of the original factor structure where n allows; otherwise EFA reported as exploratory | Short scales (PACS, 5 items) can be tested with n ≈ 100 |
| Test–retest reliability | ICC (two-way, absolute agreement) | Craving changes quickly in early abstinence, so retest **stable outpatients** over 3–7 days, not inpatients in detox |
| Convergent validity | Correlations with related constructs (PACS with OCDS; CEOA with drinking quantity; AASE with relapse history) | Prespecify expected direction and size |
| Measurement invariance (optional) | Multi-group CFA across language versions | Only if samples are large enough; a paper in itself |

Analysis in R (lavaan, psych) or JASP.

**Scope control.** Full psychometric validation is only needed for the **primary and mechanism measures** (PACS, CEOA, DTQ, AASE). For covariates with existing validated versions (PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT, WHOQOL-BREF Hindi), use the existing version and translate only the missing language with steps 1–7, reporting internal consistency in the trial sample.
