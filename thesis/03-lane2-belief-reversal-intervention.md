# 03 — Lane 2: A belief-reversal ("counter-brainwashing") intervention for alcohol dependence

## 1. What the Allen Carr method actually does

### Core propositions, in Carr's own logic (alcohol books)
1. **The trap.** Drinking relieves a low-grade discomfort that drinking itself created (the "Little Monster", withdrawal). The relief is misread as pleasure. Non-drinkers feel that way all the time; the drinker is only chasing the non-drinker's baseline (QDWW Ch 3–5; EWCA Ch 6, 16).
2. **The brainwashing (the "Big Monster").** Advertising, film, family modelling, peers and the drinker's own rationalisations install the beliefs that alcohol gives pleasure, taste, courage, confidence, relaxation, sociability and stress relief, and that quitting is hard and life without it deprived (QDWW Ch 4, 6–7; EWCA Ch 7, 11–15, 20–21, 24).
3. **Nothing to give up.** Each perceived benefit is examined and shown to be an illusion or a relief of a drug-caused problem (QDWW Ch 6–7; EWCA Ch 11–15).
4. **Fear is the jailer.** Fear of failure and fear of success both rest on illusions and are themselves caused by drinking (QDWW Ch 8, 13; EWCA Ch 13).
5. **Willpower fails by design.** It preserves the sense of sacrifice, which produces deprivation and relapse (QDWW Ch 9; EWCA Ch 5, 10, 19).
6. **Normal drinking and problem drinking are stages of one process; cutting down raises the illusion of pleasure; only total abstinence works** (QDWW Ch 11; EWCA Ch 22–23).
7. **Identity and certainty.** You become a non-drinker at the final drink, with no waiting period; never doubt the decision (QDWW Ch 12, 17; EWCA Ch 26 instructions 2, 6).
8. **Procedural instructions.** Follow all instructions in order; keep an open mind; start with elation; keep drinking while reading (EWCA Ch 1 instruction 5; QDWW Ch 1 allows it, with an exception for people already abstinent); do not cut down; ignore advice that conflicts with the method (QDWW Ch 10, instruction 5; EWCA Ch 2, 5); make a final-drink ritual and a vow (QDWW Ch 17; EWCA Ch 27); treat residual pangs as the monster dying and do nothing about them (QDWW Ch 15, 18); do not suppress thoughts of drinking, reframe them (QDWW Ch 13); write a list of genuine pleasures (QDWW Ch 13); anticipate high-risk situations and plan responses (QDWW Ch 17); go into social situations straight away (EWCA Ch 26 instruction 10); no substitutes (EWCA Ch 26 instruction 13; QDWW is softer and allows soft drinks for thirst); never envy drinkers; do not try to convert others; confess to loved ones when ready (QDWW Ch 14); quit now, not on a "meaningless day" (QDWW Ch 16).
9. **Delivery by a former user** (facilitators are described on official pages as people who used the method themselves), in a one-day group seminar with boosters, or by book, or by an on-demand video programme.

Reading: the smoking data say a one-day belief-reversal event is at least comparable to a specialist service, with weaker early abstinence and better attendance. Nobody has (a) tested the logic in alcohol, (b) tested it in a dependent clinical population, (c) measured the proposed mechanism (neither RCT measured beliefs), (d) dismantled its components, (e) adapted it to a non-Western or low-literacy setting, or (f) delivered it after medical detox, where the "keep drinking while you read" instruction is impossible. ## 2. Mapping Carr's propositions onto addiction science

Each row gives a construct to measure. Rows 1–10 are the original map; rows 11–16 were added from the alcohol books.

| # | Carr proposition (chapter) | Scientific construct | Key literature | Measurable by |

| 2 | Pleasure is an illusion; you only want it; taste is tolerance (QDWW Ch 6; EWCA Ch 12, 21) | **Incentive sensitisation: wanting ≠ liking** | Robinson & Berridge 1993–2016 | Cue VAS wanting vs liking; implicit measures |

| 4 | Fear is the jailer; fear of failure and of success (QDWW Ch 8, 13) | Anxiety sensitivity; experiential avoidance | ACT literature | AAQ-II; fear-of-abstinence items |

| 7 | "I am a non-drinker now", at the final drink (QDWW Ch 12) | **Identity change** (PRIME theory) | West 2006; Tombor et al. | Drinker identity items; single-item rulers |
| 8 | Keep drinking while learning, then one decision (EWCA Ch 1; QDWW Ch 1) | Belief updating under prediction error; memory destabilisation | Das & Kamboj; Hon et al. 2015 | Design feature only; **not transferable to dependence** (section 3) |
| 9 | Residual craving as the monster dying; don't suppress thoughts, reframe them (QDWW Ch 13, 15, 18) | Reappraisal; metacognitive beliefs about craving; desire thinking; thought-suppression rebound | Caselli & Spada; Wegner | Desire Thinking Questionnaire; metacognitions about craving |
| 10 | Delivered by a former user | Peer credibility; recorded recovery narratives | NEON (Slade, Rennick-Egglestone); KLIFAD 2023 | Narrative-connection items; engagement |
| 11 | Reattribute past failures to the method, not the person (QDWW Ch 2, 9; EWCA Ch 10) | Attribution retraining; self-efficacy repair | Bandura; Weiner | AASE; attribution items |
| 12 | Written list of genuine pleasures; written record of harms carried on the person (QDWW Ch 13; EWCA Ch 27; cannabis book instruction 6) | Reward replacement; implementation intentions; retrieval cues | Behavioural activation; Gollwitzer | Pleasure inventory completed; card carried (fidelity) |
| 13 | Pre-planned response to pangs and high-risk situations; "I want a drink → great, I'm free" (QDWW Ch 17–18; EWCA Ch 26 instructions 4–5) | Coping planning; if-then plans; relapse prevention | Marlatt; Gollwitzer | Coping-plan completeness; urge-coping self-efficacy |
| 14 | Go to parties and stressful situations straight away; don't change your lifestyle to avoid alcohol (EWCA Ch 26 instruction 10; QDWW Ch 16) | Behavioural experiments; exposure to fear of success | CBT | Social-situation diary |

Logic in one sentence: **BRI-AD is a structured, emotionally charged expectancy challenge plus identity reframe and coping-plan work, delivered with peer credibility, timed to the post-detox window, whose hypothesised proximal effects are reduced positive expectancies and desire thinking and increased abstinence self-efficacy and non-drinker identity, which in turn reduce craving and relapse.**

## 3. Departures from Carr required for a dependent, post-detox, Indian population

These are design decisions, each cited to the book it departs from. State them in the protocol.

| # | What the books say | Why it cannot stand | Protocol departure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep drinking until you finish the book, then stop abruptly with a final drink (EWCA Ch 1 instruction 5, Ch 27; QDWW Ch 1, 17). No taper, no transition period, no medical screening (QDWW Ch 13, 15) | Unsupervised abrupt cessation in dependence causes seizures and delirium | Belief work happens **after** medically supervised detox, days 5–12 of admission, CIWA-Ar low. The "final drink" becomes a symbolic commitment ceremony. |
| 2 | Withdrawal is mild, like flu; the DT fear is a myth; severe reactions come from the willpower method (QDWW Ch 15; EWCA Ch 25 says DTs are caused by drinking, not withdrawal) | Clinically false for dependent drinkers | Honest withdrawal education replaces this. The "Little Monster" metaphor is kept for the **post-detox residual urge**, not for acute withdrawal. |
| 3 | Alcohol books say **nothing** about naltrexone, acamprosate or disulfiram (QDWW: no mention anywhere); EWCA Ch 15 likens alcohol to Valium and painkillers; the cannabis book (Ch 9) is hostile to anti-anxiety medication; "ignore all advice that conflicts with the method" (QDWW instruction 5); doctors' warnings make quitting harder (EWCA Ch 5); experts can be wrong (EWCA Ch 2) | Anti-craving pharmacotherapy is evidence-based and part of treatment as usual; an intervention that undermines prescribers would fail ethics review | Medication retained and framed as "a seatbelt, not a crutch". The "ignore conflicting advice" instruction is dropped and replaced with "test every belief against your own experience and the evidence". No disparagement of clinicians, AA or mutual-help groups (QDWW Ch 1, 12 critique AA's lifelong-recovery framing; the protocol stays neutral). |
| 4 | No substitutes at all (EWCA Ch 26 instruction 13) vs soft drinks for thirst and replacing drinking with genuine pleasures (QDWW Ch 6, 11, 13) | The books disagree with each other; blanket "no substitutes" has no evidence in alcohol | Follow QDWW: non-alcoholic drinks allowed; pleasure inventory used actively (row 12). |
| 5 | A single 5–7 hour event, 20–25 people, one facilitator, plus boosters | Not deliverable to recovering inpatients with fatigue, cognitive impairment and low literacy | Five 45–60 min sessions over the admission, groups of 3–6, workbook and video, family in one session. |
| 6 | Hypnotherapy audio after the final drink (QDWW Ch 20; EWCA relaxation at the end of the seminar) | Unnecessary and raises eyebrows at IEC; the book itself says it is not the cure | Replaced by a 10-minute breathing/relaxation close in session 4. |
| 7 | "Quitting is easy", "instant cure", 90% of adults are in the trap, 10% never are (QDWW Ch 1; EWCA Ch 1; unreferenced) | Unreferenced claims; "easy" over-promises and sets up abstinence-violation reactions | Thesis claim is "removes the sense of sacrifice", not "removes discomfort". Unreferenced statistics removed. |
| 8 | Problem drinking is not caused by poverty or hardship; no addictive personality or genetic vulnerability (QDWW Ch 1, 10; EWCA Ch 18) | Overstated; heritability and adversity are real moderators | Reframed as "vulnerability is real, but it does not make the beliefs true", which preserves the de-shaming intent without denying evidence. |
| 9 | Family absent from the method | Indian alcohol dependence is managed with families; families hold their own beliefs ("he needs it to sleep") | Family joins the expectancy audit (session 2) and the commitment ceremony (session 4). |
| 10 | No screening | Psychosis, bipolar, suicidality, cognitive impairment need exclusion or deferral | MoCA, screening interview, exclusions as in `05`. |

## 4. Logic model

```
INPUTS                 ACTIVITIES (5 ward sessions + aftercare)        PROXIMAL MECHANISMS (2–4 wk)        OUTCOMES (12–24 wk)
Manual v1, videos      S1 The Trap (how wanting is built)              ↓ positive alcohol expectancies      ↓ craving (PACS, OCDS)
Facilitator + co-      S2 Dismantling my benefits (expectancy audit)   ↓ desire thinking                    ↓ relapse to heavy drinking
 facilitator in        S3 Fear, willpower, past attempts (reattribute) ↑ abstinence self-efficacy           ↑ % days abstinent
 recovery              S4 Identity, decision, ceremony                 ↑ non-drinker identity               ↑ engagement with aftercare
Workbook, family       S5 Living free (plans, "just one", pleasures)   ↓ fear of abstinence                 ↑ QoL, family burden ↓
WhatsApp aftercare     Boosters, narratives, 90-s craving tool,        ↑ coping-plan use
                       weekly check-in
```

Moderators to prespecify: severity (SADQ), baseline expectancies, literacy, family involvement, anti-craving medication at discharge.

## 5. Module architecture (v1 for the manual)

Each ward session 45–60 min, group of 3–6, days 5–12 of admission, facilitator + co-facilitator, workbook pages filled in session, 5–7 min video opens each session.

| Session | Title | Content | Carr elements (rows) | Technique | Homework |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The trap | Personal drinking timeline; first drinks vs now; the relief loop drawn as a mood graph across a drinking day; "wanting vs liking" in plain words; honest withdrawal education (departure 2) | 1, 2 | Psychoeducation, Socratic questioning | List every benefit alcohol gives you |
| 2 | Auditing the benefits | Each benefit tested: Did it exist before you drank? Does a non-drinker lack it? Does it return when you stop? Family adds its beliefs. Each classed illusion / drug-caused problem / real but replaceable. Taste-is-tolerance exercise | 3, 2, 16 | Expectancy challenge; counter-attitudinal advocacy (patient argues the case to the group) | Notice a craving and write what it promised |
| 3 | Fear, willpower and past attempts | Review of past quit attempts and reattribution to method (row 11); the deprivation story; abstinence violation effect; fear inventory (failure and success); residual urges reframed as the monster starving; medication as seatbelt (departure 3) | 4, 5, 9, 11 | Reappraisal; decisional balance with the "benefits" column now empty | Tell one family member the three illusions you found |
| 4 | The decision | Identity work: drinker → non-drinker; certainty exercise; written record of harms on a wallet card (row 12); "goodbye letter" to alcohol read aloud; family witness; 10-min breathing close (departure 6); celebration replaces the final drink (departure 1) | 6, 7, 12, 15 | Commitment, public declaration, dissonance consolidation | Record a 60-second phone video to your future self; write the pleasure list |
| 5 | Living free | Cues, festivals, social pressure, "just one" trap, scripted answers to offers; pre-planned response to pangs (row 13); "go to the function" behavioural experiments (row 14); don't envy, don't convert (row 16); relapse response without self-contempt; aftercare onboarding | 9, 13, 14, 16 | Marlatt relapse prevention reframed; if-then planning | Install/receive aftercare; carry the card |
| Aftercare (wk 1–12) | Boosters | Six 4–6 min videos (one per week for 6 weeks), 3–5 recorded recovery narratives (Assamese/Hindi/Bengali; one anonymised narrative drawn from your own experience if you choose), 90-second "remember the trap" craving tool on demand, weekly two-question check-in with facilitator reply | 10, 9, 13 | Narrative connection; spaced retrieval | — |

Delivery platform for v1: **WhatsApp broadcast + voice-note replies**. ASER 2024 reports > 90% of rural Assam households own a smartphone; a custom app adds 6–9 months and DPDP obligations. Move to the department's ANRF continuity-of-care platform in v2 once content is validated.

Fidelity: 10-item session checklist; 20% of sessions audio-recorded and rated by a second rater; facilitator manual with scripts; the co-facilitator in recovery is paid and trained.

Timing against AIIMS rules: DC by month 6, protocol to DC by month 9 from registration, so Phase 1a runs before and during protocol approval (the review and the qualitative study need only their own IEC clearance, which can go in early).

Why an attention-matched control: versus nothing, a belief-reversal intervention will "work" through attention and expectation. Psychoeducation of equal dose lets you claim a specific mechanism. Frings' finding that the NHS arm did better at 4 weeks among attenders is a warning that early effects can favour the comparator.

# Lane 2.1: BRI-AD Blend (game + video + human, with measurement built into the game)

Added 6 Nov 2026 at Dr. Reddy's request. **Lane 2.1 is not a separate thesis.** It is the delivery format of the same BRI-AD intervention: the same programme theory (§2), the same ten departures (§3), the same five sessions (§5) and the same development pathway (§7). What changes is how the content reaches the patient.

**Decisions already made (Dr. Reddy, 6 Nov 2026):**
1. **One blended intervention.** Game, video and human sessions together are BRI-AD, tested in the proof of concept and the pilot RCT. No extra arm, same sample sizes as §8.
2. **Ward tablet, then own phone.** Played on a supervised ward tablet during admission; continued on the patient's own phone after discharge as aftercare.
3. **"Testing" means measurement inside the game.** Every level also collects data on beliefs, craving, confidence and choices.
4. **Built by Dr. Reddy with a paid designer** as a web app (no app-store install); the candidate keeps control of content and can change it quickly.

This replaces "WhatsApp broadcast + voice notes" (§5) as the v1 delivery platform. WhatsApp stays as the booster channel and as the fallback if the build slips.

## 2.1-1. Why a blend, and what the evidence allows us to claim

Abstract-level reading (AB) unless marked. Not a systematic search; the Lane 2 review (`drafts/review-lane2-prospero.md`) will cover it properly.

| Source | What it shows | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Manning et al. 2021, *JAMA Psychiatry* 78(2):133–140 (4 inpatient withdrawal units, Melbourne; n = 300) | Four daily sessions of computerised approach-bias training **during inpatient withdrawal** vs sham: abstinence at the 2-week follow-up 54.4% vs 42.5%, difference 11.9% (95% CI 0.04–23.8). Seven patients withdrew because they found the training uncomfortable. **Computer tasks are deliverable in exactly our window.** | AB |
| Manning et al. 2016, *Alcohol Clin Exp Res* (pilot of the above) | 71 followed up; ITT abstinence 69% vs 47% (p = 0.07); craving did not differ | AB |
| Wiers et al. 2011, *Psychol Sci* (n = 214 inpatients); Eberl et al. 2013, *Dev Cogn Neurosci* | Four brief sessions of approach-bias retraining before inpatient treatment improved outcomes at one year; change in bias mediated the effect | AB |
| Boendermaker et al. 2016, *JMIR Serious Games* ("Shots" game) | A gamified version of attention-bias training **did not keep motivation up**; some motivation measures fell more in the game condition ("disappointing gamification" may backfire) | AB |

**What we can claim:** (a) structured computer tasks are feasible and can help in the inpatient withdrawal window; (b) expectancy challenge works without administering alcohol, which is essential in dependent patients; (c) human-delivered structured psychological treatment works in Indian settings, including by non-specialists; (d) blended human + digital formats can be delivered to severely ill patients. **What we cannot claim:** that gamification itself improves outcomes. Treat the game as the engagement and measurement vehicle; the active ingredients stay the belief work in §2. **Novelty added:** no game-based belief-reversal intervention for alcohol dependence was found in this scan, and none in an Indian language.

**Design consequence.** Computerised approach-bias training (CBM) is the best-evidenced computer task in this window. It is a different mechanism from belief reversal, so it is **not** built into v1 (adding it would make the belief mechanism impossible to test). It is listed as a v2 or factorial question.

## 2.1-4. Game design rules (from the evidence and from clinical safety)

1. **An interactive story first, points second.** Story/theme is the element mental-health programmes use most, and the Shots trial shows that points bolted onto a dull task can backfire. If playtesters do not find a level engaging, cut it rather than add rewards.
2. **No leaderboards and no public scores.** Patients play in a ward with others; shame and privacy both argue against comparison.
3. **No streaks that reset on a lapse.** A reset counter rebuilds the abstinence-violation effect that §2 row 5 targets. A reported lapse opens a "what did this teach you" level instead.
4. **Reward reflection and play, never reported abstinence.** Rewarding "days sober" would bias the in-game drinking measure.
5. **Cue safety.** Show contexts, people and consequences, not glamorous close-ups of drinks. Craving slider at the end of every level; if craving ≥ 7/10, a cool-down screen appears and, on the ward, the co-facilitator is alerted.
6. **Voice first.** Every screen read aloud in Assamese, Hindi and Bengali (English optional); icons over text; large fonts. Works for patients who do not read.
7. **No AI or chatbot in the patient-facing game.** Every word a patient sees is fixed, translated and IEC-approved. This keeps the study outside AI-specific ethics review and stops unpredictable content.
8. **Offline-first, no ads, no third-party analytics, no social login.**

## 2.1-11. Installing the whole Carr method: the path, the deck and the introspection engine

Added 6 Nov 2026 at Dr. Reddy's request: the blend should carry the **entire** Allen Carr method, not a selection, and should create deep self-examination in the patient that is sustained from the ward through aftercare. This section **expands 2.1-3**: the five game levels become five **acts**, each made of **chambers** that follow the order of *Quit Drinking Without Willpower* (QDWW). Source: the paraphrased chapter map in `local/carr-maps/quit-drinking-without-willpower.md` (machine-generated, not yet checked chapter by chapter by a human: do that check before the storyboard is frozen).

### A. Three principles taken from how the method actually works

1. **It is a combination lock.** Carr's first instruction is to follow every step in order, because each illusion can only fall once the one before it has fallen (QDWW Ch 1). The game therefore **locks the chambers in order**, and the final ceremony unlocks only when the patient's own belief file shows the core illusions have been seen through (Carr's pre-quit checklist and "reread what you have not understood", Ch 16).
2. **It works on the person's own beliefs, not on information.** Carr argues with the reader's reasons one by one. The game does the same with a **personal deck** of the patient's own belief cards, in their own words, collected in Phase 1a and at baseline.
3. **It ends every step in relief, not shame.** Carr's third instruction is to start with elation; addiction is described as a trap, not a character flaw (Ch 3). Every chamber in the game closes on release ("you saw through it"), never on guilt.

### B. The path: QDWW condensed into 15 chambers (0–14) across the five acts

Each chamber is 12–20 minutes on the ward tablet. Total game time is about 3½ to 4½ hours over days 5–12, plus 5 human sessions (about 5 hours). That is close to the dose of a one-day Carr seminar (4.5–6 hours, `03` §1), but spread over a week so that each illusion has a night to settle.

### C. The Five Tests (the question chain applied to every belief card)

Carr dismantles each illusion with the same few questions. The game makes them a fixed chain the patient answers, by voice or tap, for every card:

1. **Before:** Did you have this before you ever drank?
2. **Others:** Do people who never drink lack it?
3. **After:** What happens to it when the drink wears off, and the next morning?
4. **Source:** Who taught you that alcohol gives this?
5. **Cost:** What has getting it this way cost you?

Then: **"What do you see now?"** The patient sets the card's status. All questions are **concrete ("what", "when", "who") rather than abstract ("why am I like this")**, because concrete processing is the constructive form of repetitive thought and abstract self-questioning is the form linked to brooding (Watkins 2008, *Psychol Bull* 134:163–206; SEC).

### D. The introspection engine: how depth is created and kept going

**The Belief Ledger.** One persistent file per patient, holding every belief card with the patient's own words and its status:

`Held → Questioned → Tested → Seen through` (and `Returned`, if the belief comes back later)

Every answer to the Five Tests is stored on the card as its evidence trail. The ledger is the spine of the whole programme: the human sessions, the chambers, the ceremony check and the aftercare all read from it.

**Five mechanisms that create depth:**

| Mechanism | What happens | Carr source |
|---|---|---|
| Own words | Cards are written from the patient's own reasons (baseline interview + Phase 1a catalogue), not from a generic list | Ch 7 (works through the reader's own reasons) |
| The Mirror | The game plays back the patient's earlier answers at the right moment: "On day 6 you said alcohol helps you sleep. You have slept five nights without it. What do you see now?" Only the patient's own answers, never other people's | Ch 2 (perception and preconception) |
| Origins | For each card: where it came from (person, place, film, festival); builds a personal map of how the beliefs were installed | Ch 4 (the brainwashing) |
| Witness | Watching drinkers in scenarios and judging, honestly, whether they are happy and in control | Ch 17 |
| Experiments | The visual illusion, the white-bear test, the door-hinge game: the patient discovers the point in their own experience before it is named | Ch 2, 9, 13 |

**Five mechanisms that sustain it after discharge:**

| Mechanism | Schedule | Carr source |
|---|---|---|
| **Urge log** ("what did this urge promise?" → reframe → status of the related card) | Any time, 30–60 s; prompted once a day at the patient's own risk time in weeks 1–4 | Ch 13, 15, 18 |

**The human sessions become "doubt clinics".** Each session opens with the group's returned or stuck cards (shown anonymously on the group screen; never named). The facilitator works through them the way a Carr therapist answers doubts in the seminar; the co-facilitator in recovery says how the same belief fell for them. One-to-one time is given to any patient whose ledger shows the same card stuck after two passes.

### E. Depth without harm: safety rails for intensive self-examination

The request is for an extreme form of introspection. The programme should be **intensive**, but there is a real clinical risk here and the protocol must say how it is handled: early abstinence carries raised risk of low mood, shame and suicidal thinking, and open-ended self-questioning can tip into brooding rather than insight (brooding vs reflection: Treynor et al. 2003, *Cognit Ther Res* 27:247–259; SEC). In the protocol and the PIS, call it **"intensive structured self-examination"**; the word "extreme" will alarm an IEC.

1. **Structure, not open-endedness.** Every introspection step is a fixed question chain (C) that ends with closure. No free-floating "reflect on your life" screens.
2. **Time-boxed.** Chambers are capped at 20 minutes; evening reflection at 5 minutes; the game stops offering new chambers after 9 pm.
3. **Relief at the end of every chamber.** A chamber cannot end on a guilt or loss screen.
4. **Mood and craving gate.** Mood and craving sliders at the end of every chamber. Low mood or craving ≥ 7/10 → cool-down screen and staff alert on the ward; in aftercare, a same-day call. Baseline suicide-risk screening by a clinician (C-SSRS or the department's standard), repeated if a mood flag fires. The game itself never asks about suicide; a human does.
5. **De-shaming throughout.** Carr's framing (it is a trap, not a weakness) is kept on every screen that touches past failures.
6. **Measure the harm side.** Add the **RRS brooding and reflection subscales** (10 items; Treynor 2003) at baseline, discharge, 4 and 12 weeks: brooding is the safety signal, reflection a mechanism. Optional: the **Self-Reflection and Insight Scale** (Grant et al. 2002, *Soc Behav Pers* 30:821–835; SEC) as the insight mechanism. Both need translation (add to `drafts/scale-translation-plan.md` Tier 2) and permission checks.

Carr's **ideas** (the trap, the illusions, the order of the argument) can be used and cited. His **wording, metaphor names and branding** should not be. The "Little Monster" and "Big Monster" names are distinctive Carr expression: give them **our own names, chosen with patients** in the paper-prototype round. Working proposals: **"Maya"** (the installed illusion; the word carries the sense of illusion in both Hindi and Assamese) and **"the Hook"** (the residual urge). ### H. The illusion deck: covering the whole book

QDWW's map lists about 50 illusions in book order (`local/carr-maps/`). Each becomes a candidate card, with three fates:

1. **Kept** (most): written in our own words and tested against the Phase 1a catalogue; local versions added (rice beer and hospitality, work stamina, cold and pain, sleep, festivals).
2. **Reframed** (where a departure applies): poverty and hardship as causes, addictive personality and genetics (departure 8); "withdrawal is mild" (departure 2); "ignore doctors" (departure 3).
3. **Dropped** with a stated reason: unreferenced statistics (departure 7).

## 2.1-12. Feasibility and practicality: core blend for the PhD, full blend after it

Added 6 Nov 2026 after a feasibility review requested by Dr. Reddy. **Verdict: the full design (2.1-1 to 2.1-11) is too large for one PhD done alongside a full-time Project Research Scientist post. A core version of the same programme fits the AIIMS clock if ward numbers allow.** The full version becomes the post-PhD grant or product.

### A. Why the full version does not fit

**1. Participants.** The full plan needs about 370 people with alcohol dependence from one ward:

| Step | Patients needed |
|---|---|
| Qualitative study (Phase 1a) | 20–25 (plus 10 family, 8 staff) |
| Translation: cognitive interviews | about 20 |
| Translation: separate psychometric field tests (≥ 100 per language, 2 languages) | about 200 |
| Usability testing | 20–30 |
| Proof of concept | 30 |
| Pilot RCT | 64–80 |
| **Total** | **about 370** |

At the pack's planning rate of **about 6 consenting patients a month** (20 detox admissions, half eligible, 60% consent; real numbers still unknown, open question 5), 370 patients take about **5 years**, which is the maximum registration period (minimum 3 years, `local/04-aiims-rules.md`). Without the separate field tests the total is about 170, or roughly 28 months of recruitment spread across phases. That fits, but only just.

**2. Ward time.** The 15 chambers need about 3½–4½ hours of play plus 5 hours of human sessions between days 5 and 12, which assumes a stay of at least 12 days. If mean stay is 7–10 days, the full path does not fit on the ward.

**3. Content load, more than code.** The app itself is buildable by Dr. Reddy. The real work is writing 15 chambers of scripts, translating and voicing them in three languages, filming, and annexing every screen in every language for the IEC, where each later change needs an amendment.

**4. Roles that cannot be one person.** Designer, builder, facilitator, assessor and analyst cannot all be the candidate: the assessor must be blinded, and reviewers will flag developer–evaluator bias. The study needs a paid co-facilitator in recovery, a separate blinded assessor and a designer.

**5. Unresolved approvals and resources.** Medical-device status of the software (2.1-6); funding (open question 9); whether CBD-trial participants may join (open question 6); commercial intent (2.1-10).

### B. The core blend (what the thesis tests)

| Component | In the PhD? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Lane 2 review (paper 1) and qualitative study (paper 2) | Yes | As drafted in `drafts/` |
| **All 15 chambers of the Carr method** | Yes, in mixed form | The facilitator delivers every chamber in the five sessions with a **printed card deck and workbook**; the full method is preserved |
| **Digital core** | Yes | Belief Ledger; Five Tests on every card; urge log; spaced card resurfacing to week 12; *Who Drank?* video; 3–4 key mini-games (seeing-differently demo, Day Graph, door-hinge, white-bear test); mood and craving gate |
| Languages | 2 fully, the third if needed | Choose after the ward's actual languages are known (open question 7) |
| Questionnaire validation | Reduced | Forward/back translation and cognitive interviews for the core scales (PACS, CEOA, AASE, RRS); reliability reported in the proof-of-concept and RCT samples instead of separate 100-person field tests. Existing validated versions used for covariates |
| Proof of concept (n = 30) | Yes | Feasibility, acceptability, engagement, ledger and in-game measure validity |
| Pilot RCT (n = 64–80, 12-week follow-up) | Yes | Endpoint of the thesis; framed as feasibility plus mechanism, not efficacy |
| Fully digital 15-chamber game in 3 languages with full voice and video | **No** | Post-PhD grant or product, built on the thesis data |

**Participants for the core blend:** about 170 (qualitative 20–25, cognitive interviews about 20, usability 20–30, proof of concept 30, pilot RCT 64–80).

### C. Timeline for the core blend (months from registration)

| Months | Work | Gate |
|---|---|---|

Publication gate (2 papers with IF > 1.5, or 1 with IF > 3): the review and the qualitative paper can be submitted by about month 18, well before the thesis.

### D. Facts that decide whether even the core blend fits

1. **Detox admissions per month and mean length of stay** (open question 5). Below about 6 consenting patients a month, cut further (for example, proof of concept plus a smaller feasibility RCT).
2. **Co-enrolment with the CBD trial** (open question 6). If trial participants are excluded, the eligible pool may shrink sharply.
3. **Protected PhD hours per week** alongside the Project Research Scientist post.
4. **Budget and people:** designer, paid co-facilitator in recovery, blinded assessor (open questions 8 and 9).
5. **Languages actually spoken on the ward** (open question 7).
6. **Commercial intent** for the game (2.1-10).

### E. What stays for after the PhD

The fully digital 15-chamber game in three languages, full voice and video production, separate psychometric validation studies, 24-week and longer follow-up, a definitive RCT (N ≈ 110–160, §8), and a dismantling study of which chambers matter (using the fidelity and ledger data from the thesis).
